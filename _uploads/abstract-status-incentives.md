# Status Incentives and Career Motivation: Evidence from International Chess Players

## Abstract

Can receiving higher career status motivate better performance? The key difficulty in answering this question lies in separating the effect of status itself from the performance that typically earns it (Frey and Gallus, 2017). I address this selection problem using the FIDE Rating Compression reform among chess players, which allows me to identify an externally induced gain in chess players' career status unrelated to their prior performance. I find that players who received higher career status subsequently showed an increase in career motivation, as they moved further up the career-status ladder, driven by improvements in performance outcomes. Evidence from in-game moves further reveals that these performance improvements come from changes in the quality of play, as players made fewer consequential mistakes within games. The evidence is consistent with a status-motivation mechanism whereby a higher position on the status ladder raises the performance benchmark associated with that position and individuals respond by performing up to the newly assigned benchmark. These results suggest that career status, usually assigned as a reward for past performance, can also be used as an incentive tool to motivate future performance.
