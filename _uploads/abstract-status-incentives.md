# Status Incentives and Career Motivation: Evidence from International Chess Players

## Abstract

Can receiving higher status in career motivate better performance? The key difficulty in answering this lies in separating the effect of status itself from the performance that typically earns it (Frey and Gallus, 2017). I address this selection problem by studying the FIDE Rating Compression reform among chess players as a setting; which allows me to identify an externally induced gain in chess player's career status unrelated to their prior performance. I find players who received higher career-status thereafter showed an increase in career-motivation, as they subsequently moved further up the career-status ladder, driven through improvement in performance outcomes. Moves played in-games further reveal these performance improvements comes from changes in their quality of play, as players made fewer consequential mistakes within games. The evidence works inline with status-motivation mechanism, where a higher position on the status ladder raises the performance benchmark associated with that position, underlying which individuals respond by performing up to the newly assigned benchmark. These results suggests that status in career, usually assigned as a reward for past performance, can also be used as an incentive-tool to motivate future performance.
